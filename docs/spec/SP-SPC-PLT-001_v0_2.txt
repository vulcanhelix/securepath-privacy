Executive Summary
SecurePath requires a white-label, multi-tenant platform that MSP partners and end-user organisations use to implement privacy and cybersecurity frameworks and then maintain them every month. Partners sell it under their own brand as a managed compliance service; end users can also buy it directly from SecurePath.

Keep each partner and client strictly separate; give partners one console across all their clients; run every client programme from a versioned framework template (POPIA and PAIA, UK GDPR and DPA 2018, ISO/IEC 27001, ISO/IEC 27701, Cyber Essentials and its South African adaptation); close findings only on accepted evidence; and generate the Monthly Maintenance cycle and monthly report under the partner’s brand.
Where it comes from. The design is taken from a POPIA programme already running in Excel for a South African client. That programme becomes the first template and the first client to migrate.
What the developer is asked to do. Propose the stack, hosting provider and South African and UK regions for a hosted multi-tenant web application; price release 1; show how the tenant isolation, audit and residency requirements in section 12 are met; and raise any requirement that cannot be met as written.
Open points. Fifteen decisions are listed in section 15. Four affect the developer’s contract and quote: intellectual property assignment to SecurePath, the product name, the licence model and the planning load.

1. Document control and purpose
This specification sets out what the SecurePath Compliance Platform must do, so that a developer can estimate, build and test it. The platform lets managed service providers (MSPs) and end-user organisations implement privacy and cybersecurity frameworks and then maintain them month by month. It is sold under each partner's own brand.
Conventions
Requirement identifiers take the form FR-<module>-<number> for functional requirements and NFR-<number> for non-functional requirements.
Priority follows MoSCoW: Must is required for release 1, Should is expected where effort allows, Could may be deferred.
Partner means an MSP or reseller that sells and delivers the platform to its own clients. Client means an organisation whose compliance programme runs on the platform. Programme means one framework implementation for one client.
Role names that carry a legal meaning (Information Officer, Deputy Information Officer, Data Protection Officer) change with the jurisdiction (section 5.4).
Figures on the screen designs in Annex B are illustrative and are not client data.
2. Product overview and scope
SecurePath owns the platform and its framework content. MSP partners resell it under their own brand and use it to deliver compliance as a managed service. End-user organisations can also buy it directly from SecurePath. Each client runs one or more framework programmes: first an implementation phase that closes the gaps found at assessment, then a monthly maintenance cycle with no end date.

3. Platform model
The platform has four levels: SecurePath, partners, clients and programmes. Each level sees only what sits beneath it.

Figure 1: Platform model
SecurePath runs the platform, publishes the framework templates, issues partner licences and supports partners. It sees partner and licence data. It sees a client's programme data only through logged, time-limited support access that the client has approved (NFR-04).
Partners brand the platform, onboard clients, start programmes from templates and deliver the work. A partner sees only its own clients. SecurePath Direct is SecurePath acting as the partner for clients who buy directly.
Clients own their programme data. A client can have programmes under more than one framework, and evidence can be shared between them where controls overlap (FR-TPL-07).
Programmes are one framework for one client, created from a template version. Each runs an implementation phase, then a monthly maintenance cycle with no end date.

5.3 Requirements
FR-TPL-01 (Must): a content author creates and edits templates in a draft state, and the platform admin publishes them as a numbered version.
FR-TPL-02 (Must): a programme records the template version it was created from. Publishing a new version never changes running programmes.
FR-TPL-03 (Must): when a new version is published, partners see what changed and can apply it to selected programmes. Applying it adds and updates items without losing client data, progress or evidence.
FR-TPL-04 (Must): partners can add, edit or retire activities, findings, KPIs and maintenance tasks within a programme without changing the template.
FR-TPL-05 (Should): partners can save their own template variants, which stay private to that partner.
FR-TPL-06 (Must): import and export templates as structured files, so content can be authored in a spreadsheet.
FR-TPL-07 (Should): a mapping table links requirements across frameworks, so evidence accepted in one programme can be offered for a mapped requirement in another programme for the same client. Acceptance is still made separately in each programme.
FR-TPL-08 (Must): no legal period, deadline or statutory reference is hard-coded. All are parameters in the jurisdiction pack or template, entered by a content author.
5.4 Jurisdiction packs
A jurisdiction pack holds the values that change with the law: role labels, regulator name, response periods for data subject requests, breach notification rules and periods, public holidays, date and currency formats, and the default data region. Release 1 needs a South Africa pack and a United Kingdom pack. SecurePath enters and verifies every value against primary sources before publication; the developer supplies the structure only.
5.5 Content rules
ISO standards are copyright material. Templates may cite clause and control numbers and use SecurePath's own wording, but must not reproduce the standards' text.
The use of the Cyber Essentials name for the South African adaptation needs a trade mark and scheme-rules check before release (section 15).
The platform supports work towards a standard or law. Nothing in the product, its reports or its marketing may state or imply that a client is certified or compliant because it uses the platform.

FR-MNT-07 (Must): the monthly report cannot go to review while a task feeding it is incomplete, unless the programme lead records a reason that prints in the report.
FR-MNT-08 (Must): tasks with an active-from rule start only once the named activity is complete.

9. Business rules and calculations
The rules below apply to every programme. Thresholds are programme settings with the defaults shown; legal periods come from the jurisdiction pack.
9.1 Dates, weeks, months and phases
Value
Rule
Week start date
Programme start date + (week − 1) × 7 days
Activity or finding due date
Programme start date + (end week − 1) × 7 + 4 days (the Friday of the end week)
Programme month of an activity
Calendar month of (due date − 4 days), counted from the start month as 1
Phase
From the template's phase boundaries by end week
Current programme week
Whole weeks from start date to report date, plus 1, within the programme's length
Business day
Monday to Friday, excluding public holidays in the jurisdiction pack calendar
Report due date

9.2 Activity RAG
Evaluated in this order; the first rule that applies sets the RAG.
Status Complete: Complete.
Status Blocked: Red.
Past due and not Deferred: Red if more than the Red threshold (default 14 days) past due, otherwise Amber.
Status At risk: Amber.
Start date reached: Green.
Otherwise: Not due.
9.3 Register status and days overdue
Days overdue = report date − planned close date, never below zero, blank once Closed.
Status values: Not started, In progress, At risk, Blocked, Closed. Closed is set only by 9.4.
9.4 Register closure
A finding becomes Closed only when every linked activity is Complete and the accountable officer, or the named alternate, has accepted evidence for it. The platform records the date, the user and the evidence references. Re-opening needs the accountable officer and a reason.
9.5 Monthly snapshots
On the last business day of each month the platform stores a snapshot of every monthly figure for each programme. Reports are built from the snapshot, so an issued report cannot change afterwards.
9.6 Workstream, programme and partner RAG (proposed)
Workstream RAG: the worst RAG among its started, incomplete activities; the programme lead may override with a reason.
Programme RAG: set monthly by the programme lead; the platform suggests the worst workstream RAG.
Partner console RAG: the programme RAG, shown Red regardless if the monthly report is late or a maintenance task feeding it is overdue.
9.7 Operational deadlines
Data subject request response periods, opt-out deadlines and breach notification rules are read from the jurisdiction pack, with any framework-specific targets from the template. Reminders run off those values.
9.8 Licence counting

12. Non-functional requirements
The platform holds clients' personal information and security evidence for many organisations at once, so tenant isolation and auditability come before every other requirement. SecurePath acts as an operator (POPIA) or processor (UK GDPR) for each client, usually through a partner; the contract chain is set out in 12.3.
12.1 Tenant isolation and access
NFR-01 (Must): every query is filtered by partner and client on the server. No user can reach another partner's or client's data by changing a URL, an ID or an API call. Isolation is covered by automated tests on every release.
NFR-02 (Must): evidence files are stored per client, encrypted at rest, and served only through short-lived signed links after a permission check.
NFR-03 (Must): single sign-on with Microsoft Entra ID and Google for organisations that use them; MFA for every account.
NFR-04 (Must): SecurePath staff have no standing access to client data; access only through a support access grant (FR-PLT-04).
NFR-05 (Must): sessions time out after a configurable idle period (default 30 minutes).
NFR-06 (Must): uploaded files are scanned for malware before storage.
NFR-07 (Must): independent penetration test, including tenant isolation, before launch and yearly after.
12.2 Audit and integrity
NFR-08 (Must): an append-only audit trail of every change at every level that no user, including platform admins, can edit or delete.
NFR-09 (Must): accepted evidence and signed reports are locked; later changes create new versions.
NFR-10 (Must): timestamps stored in UTC and shown in the client's time zone.
12.3 Privacy, residency and contracts
NFR-11 (Must): each client's data is stored in a selected region: South Africa or the United Kingdom in release 1. Cross-border transfers must meet POPIA section 72 or the UK GDPR transfer rules, as applicable.
NFR-12 (Must): the developer, hosting provider and any sub-processors are listed and contracted as SecurePath's sub-operators or sub-processors.
NFR-13 (Must): the contract chain runs SecurePath to partner (platform agreement with data processing terms), then partner to client. Direct clients contract with SecurePath.
NFR-14 (Must): on client exit, a full export is provided and the data is deleted within a configurable period, with a deletion certificate.
NFR-15 (Must): awareness check results are stored and reported only as averages.
12.4 Availability, scale and support
NFR-16 (Must): 99.5% availability measured monthly, with planned maintenance outside South African and UK business hours.
NFR-17 (Must): daily backups per region with a restoration test each quarter.
NFR-18 (Should): pages load in under 3 seconds at the planning load of \[number\] partners and \[number\] programmes, to be set by SecurePath.
NFR-19 (Must): current Edge, Chrome and Safari; phones at 390 pixels wide.
NFR-20 (Should): WCAG 2.1 level AA.
12.5 Build approach
The Microsoft 365 option in draft 0.1 suited one client. It does not fit a white-labelled, multi-partner product: each client would need its own SharePoint build, partners could not have one console across clients, and branding would be limited. Release 1 is therefore specified as a hosted, multi-tenant web application. The developer is asked to propose the stack, hosting provider and regions, and to show how NFR-01 to NFR-14 are met.
13. Onboarding, import and the first client migration
A new client's findings come from one of three sources: the template defaults, an assessment file, or the client's existing tracker. The first live client arrives through the third route.
13.1 Import routes
FR-IMP-01 (Must): import findings from a structured spreadsheet (reference, finding, priority, assessment window, lead) and map them to the template's activities.
FR-IMP-02 (Should): import assessment results from SecurePath's assessment workbook, where each failed control maps to a template finding.
FR-IMP-03 (Could): in a later release, receive readiness check results from partner portals by API.
FR-IMP-04 (Must): import an existing programme tracker in the Excel layout described in 13.2.
FR-IMP-05 (Must): every import shows a preview and a list of rows rejected with reasons before anything is written.
